Five layers, one simulated

The sensor layer is replaced by a hardware-in-the-loop simulator that emits the same JSON schema at the same rate over the same transport an ESP32 and ELM327 dongle would. Everything above it is a real implementation running on that live data flow.

L1
Data collection
GPS, OBD-II and fuel sensing at 1 Hz per vehicle. Road-load physics generates ground truth; sensor noise and SAE J1979 quantisation are applied on top.

What changes when the hardware arrives

One class. adapters/base.py defines TelemetryAdapter.stream(); nothing above layer 1 may read a field outside schema v1.0, and validate_packet() enforces that at the boundary on every packet. adapters/esp32_adapter.py carries the full PID poll sequence, the MQTT topic layout and the offline buffering strategy, and raises NotImplementedError rather than returning anything plausible.

Not a placeholder. The stub documents the ELM327 poll order, the decode for each of the ten PIDs, what to do on a vehicle that does not support PID 0x5E, and why payload mass has to be joined in from dispatch rather than read from the bus.

Why the simulator is physics, not random numbers

Ground truth comes from the road-load equation used by MOVES and COPERT, with a Willans line for the engine and an air-path model for PID 0x04. Fuel is never drawn from a distribution.

The check that keeps it honest. scripts/gate_a_physics.py refuses to pass if any single telemetry field can reconstruct the fuel rate on its own. Two earlier versions of the engine model failed it, which is how they were caught.
